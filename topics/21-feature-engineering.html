Feature Engineering & Data Processing
The stage where domain knowledge actually enters the model — and where essentially every leak is introduced, because the useful features are aggregates and every aggregate is a chance to let the label or the future into the input. A model that leaks scores beautifully offline and loses money in production, so the discipline here is worth more than the cleverness.
Target encoding: the highest-leverage feature and the easiest leak
Replace a category with the mean of the target for that category. It collapses a 10,000-level column into one dense, informative number and it routinely beats every alternative on tabular data. It is also the fastest way to leak the label into a feature, and the failure is not subtle once you see it.
Consider a category that appears exactly once. Its "mean target" is computed from one row — that row. So the encoded feature for that row is its own label, exactly. With 5,000 cities across 10,000 rows, a large share are singletons, and you have handed the model a partial copy of y. Cross-validation looks superb, production is a coin flip, and nothing in the code looks wrong.
Two fixes, and you need both. Smoothing blends the category mean toward the global mean, weighted by how much evidence the category actually has:
$$\text{encoded} = \frac{n_c \cdot \bar{y}_c + m \cdot \bar{y}_{\text{global}}}{n_c + m}$$
where nc is the category's row count, ȳc its target mean, and m a smoothing weight — read it as "how many rows of prior I am pretending to have". With a 5% global default rate and m = 20:
CategoryRaw encodingSmoothed (m = 20)Effect
1 row, that row defaulted1.000 — the label itself0.095pulled almost all the way back to the 5% prior
100 rows, 10 defaulted0.1000.092barely moved — the evidence is real
A single default nudges a thin category from 5% to 9.5% rather than to 100%. Regularisation is strongest exactly where evidence is thinnest — the same shape as λ dominating a small leaf's denominator in XGBoost.
Smoothing alone is not enough, though, because a row still contributes to the statistic that becomes its own feature. Out-of-fold encoding closes that: split into folds, and encode each fold using only the other folds' rows. A row never sees itself. This is what TargetEncoder in recent sklearn and CatBoost's ordered statistics both do, and it is the only version that belongs anywhere near production.

Leakage: three kinds, one question that catches them
Leakage is the defining failure of this topic and it is worth naming the varieties, because they are found in different ways.
KindWhat happensExample
Target leakagea feature encodes the outcome — it exists because the label happenedn_collection_calls when predicting default; discharge_code when predicting readmission. Available in your warehouse, not available at decision time
Train–test contaminationa statistic computed across the splitscaler, imputer, target encoder or feature selector fitted before splitting. The mechanism that makes Pipeline non-optional
Temporal leakagean aggregate whose window includes the futurecustomer_lifetime_value as a feature for a purchase in month 3. Also any shuffled CV on time-ordered rows
the two-part test for every feature

For each column ask: (1) at the moment of prediction, would this value exist? and (2) would it have this value? The first catches obviously unavailable fields. The second is the one that finds real bugs — account_status exists at scoring time, but the value in your training table is today's status, not the status as of the decision. Same column name, different number, and the difference is the leak.

Symptoms to act on: a depth-2 tree reaching 0.99 AUC; one feature holding most of the importance; validation far above anything the business thought possible; and the definitive one — CV at 0.95 and production at 0.62. A shallow tree is the cheapest leak detector available and it names the guilty column for you.

Features that repeatedly earn their place
  • Ratios over raw pairs. Debt-to-income, click-through rate, price per square metre. A linear model cannot construct a quotient and a tree needs many splits to approximate one, so handing over the ratio directly is often the largest single gain available. Divide by the thing that makes rows comparable is most of feature engineering
  • Lags and differences for anything temporal — value last week, change since last month. This is also the fix for the tree-extrapolation problem: a tree cannot predict a level it has never seen, but a difference stays inside its training range even as the level grows
  • Velocity and rolling counts. Transactions in the last hour, distinct merchants in seven days, failed logins in ten minutes. In fraud these are usually the top features outright, because the signal is a change in rate rather than any single value. They are also the ones most often computed with a leaking window — the window must end strictly before the event
  • Cyclical encoding for periodic quantities. Hour 23 and hour 0 are one hour apart and numerically 23 apart, so a raw hour column tells the model midnight is maximally distant from 11pm. Map to a circle instead:
$$x_{\sin} = \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi h}{24}\right), \qquad x_{\cos} = \cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi h}{24}\right)$$
Check that it works. Hour 0 sits at (0.000, 1.000), hour 1 at (0.259, 0.966), hour 23 at (−0.259, 0.966). The distance from midnight to 1am is 0.261; from midnight to 11pm it is also 0.261 — correctly symmetric, where the raw column made one of them 23 times further. Noon lands at distance 2.000, the maximum. Same for day-of-week, month, and wind direction.
  • The missingness indicator. Nearly free and frequently more predictive than the column it describes. If income is blank for 8% of applicants and those applicants default at 12% against a 4% base rate, then income_is_missing beats income. Impute and keep the flag — imputing alone throws the signal away
  • Explicit interactions where the domain implies them — hour-of-day × day-of-week, user_segment × product_category. Trees can find these given depth and data; stating them saves both. Scale the components first, or the wider-ranged one dominates the product

Watch for levels that appear at inference and never appeared in training — a new city, a new device model. Decide the fallback deliberately (global mean for target encoding, a reserved bucket for one-hot) or the first unseen value will throw in production.
Scaling: who needs it and why
ModelScale matters?Because
k-NN, SVM, k-means, PCAcriticallythe objective is a distance or a variance, so units decide which features exist
Regularised linear / logisticyesthe penalty charges per unit of coefficient, and coefficient size is inverse to feature scale
Neural networksyesconditioning — see below — plus saturating activations
Trees, RF, XGBoostnosplits use order only, so any monotonic transform gives an identical model
The gradient-descent reason, which is less often stated. Wildly different feature scales make the loss surface a long narrow valley, and the number of gradient steps to converge grows linearly with the condition number of the Hessian. Since that condition number tracks the ratio of feature variances, a 1000:1 spread in standard deviations turns a few hundred steps into a few hundred thousand. Standardising is not cosmetic; it is what makes the optimiser's job tractable.
Standardise (subtract mean, divide by sd) by default. Min-max when you need bounded input, accepting that one outlier compresses everything else. Robust scaling (median and IQR) when outliers are real and you want to keep them without letting them set the scale.
Feature selection — and the leak hiding in it

Mutual informationfilternon-linear dependence with the target, unlike a correlation filter

RFE / Borutawrapperaccurate and expensive; refits the model repeatedly
selection is model fitting, so it belongs inside the fold

Pick the best 20 of 500 features on the full dataset, then cross-validate the model on those 20, and your CV score is contaminated — the selection already read every row, including the ones each fold is about to be validated on.

The demonstration is stark. Take 100 rows and 500 columns of pure noise with a random binary target. Keep the 10 columns most correlated with y, then cross-validate. You will get an accuracy comfortably above 0.5 from data containing no signal whatsoever, because the selection step found the columns that happened to correlate with the labels in this sample. Put the selector inside the Pipeline and the score returns to chance, which is the correct answer.

A practical order for 500 features: drop near-constant columns, prune correlations above 0.95, rank the rest by SHAP on one fitted model, then remove from the bottom while watching CV — and stop when it drops. Cheaper than RFE, faster than Boruta, and honest as long as every step sits inside the fold.
Mistakes that survive code review
  • Target encoding on the full dataset. The canonical leak. Singleton categories become copies of the label. Out-of-fold plus smoothing, always
  • Any transform fitted before the split. Scalers, imputers, encoders, selectors, PCA. All of them must refit inside each fold, which is what Pipeline is for and why hand-rolled preprocessing is where leaks live
  • Aggregates over windows that include the event. "Average spend" computed over all history, used to predict a transaction inside that history. The window must close before the timestamp you are predicting — and it must close at the same offset it will have in production
  • One-hot encoding a 10,000-level column. Ten thousand near-empty columns. Target, frequency, hashing, or embeddings
  • Log-transforming without checking for zeros. log(0) is −∞ and will propagate NaN through your whole matrix. Use log1p, and remember to invert it correctly if you transformed the target
  • Imputing without a flag. Mean imputation converts "we do not know" into "this person is average", which is both false and less useful than the fact of the gap
  • Interactions on unscaled features. x₁·x₂ is dominated by whichever has the wider range, so the product mostly encodes one input
  • Building features the production system cannot compute. A 30-day rolling distinct count is trivial in a notebook and needs a feature store with the right freshness guarantee at serving time. Training on a feature you cannot serve is a very expensive way to discover an architectural constraint
  • Train/serve skew. The same feature computed by two different code paths — SQL for training, application code for serving — will disagree eventually. Compute both from one definition, or monitor the distributions of both
How this is done at scale
  • Velocity features are the fraud stack. Counts and distinct-counts over rolling windows — transactions in the last hour, distinct merchants in seven days, failed attempts in ten minutes — served from a low-latency store at inference. The engineering problem is guaranteeing that the window available online matches the window used in training, because a few minutes of difference is enough to inflate offline results
  • Feature stores exist to solve exactly one problem: point-in-time correctness. Given an event at time t, return the feature values as they were at t rather than as they are now. Doing this by hand in SQL is possible and is where temporal leaks come from, which is why the tooling category exists at all
  • Composite quality scores. Marketplaces build single engineered features — a listing quality score combining review count, response rate and conversion — that outperform their raw inputs in ranking, because they encode a business judgement about how the inputs trade off that the model would need far more data to learn
  • Cross features in ranking. Explicit user × content crosses (user_industry × post_topic) fed as sparse inputs remain standard alongside learned embeddings. They give a linear or wide component the interaction terms it cannot form, which is the entire idea behind wide-and-deep architectures
  • Temporal features survive deep learning. Even in neural forecasting stacks, hand-built lags, rolling statistics and Fourier seasonality terms stay in the input. The network could in principle learn them; given finite data it does better when handed them
🎯 In an interview

Lead with leakage, not with cleverness. Anyone can list encodings. Say that your first pass on any feature is the two-part test — would this value exist at prediction time, and would it have this value — and that you fit every transform inside the CV fold via a Pipeline. That answer is what distinguishes someone who has shipped a model from someone who has trained one.

Expect: "How do you encode a high-cardinality categorical?" — Target encoding, then immediately name the trap: a singleton category's encoding is its own label. Fix with smoothing toward the global mean, weighted by category count, and with out-of-fold computation so a row never contributes to its own feature. Quoting the smoothing formula and a number (5% prior, m = 20, one default → 0.095 not 1.0) settles the question.

Expect: "You have 500 features, what do you do?" — Variance filter, correlation prune above 0.95, SHAP rank, then trim from the bottom while watching CV. Then volunteer the part most candidates miss: selection is model fitting, so doing it outside the fold contaminates the score — 500 noise columns and 100 rows will beat chance if you select first and validate second.

Good things to volunteer: that a missingness indicator is often stronger than the imputed column; that cyclical features need sin/cos or midnight is 23 hours from 11pm; and that trees cannot extrapolate, so for trending targets you engineer differences rather than levels.

In code
Target encoding is the highest-leverage tabular feature and the easiest way to leak. The out-of-fold version is the only one that belongs in production.
import numpy as np, pandas as pd
from sklearn.model_selection import KFold

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
df = pd.DataFrame({'city': rng.choice(list('ABCDEFGH'), 5000),
                   'y': rng.random(5000) < .2})

# WRONG: the mean of a row's own target leaks straight into its feature
df['te_leaky'] = df.groupby('city')['y'].transform('mean')

# RIGHT: out-of-fold, so a row's encoding never sees its own label,
# with smoothing toward the global mean for rare categories
prior, k = df.y.mean(), 20
df['te'] = np.nan
for tr, va in KFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=0).split(df):
    stats = df.iloc[tr].groupby('city')['y'].agg(['mean', 'count'])
    smooth = (stats['mean'] * stats['count'] + prior * k) / (stats['count'] + k)
    df.loc[df.index[va], 'te'] = df.iloc[va]['city'].map(smooth).fillna(prior)

print(df.groupby('city')[['te_leaky', 'te']].mean().round(4))

# Other staples: cyclical time, ratios, and winsorising instead of dropping
t = pd.Series(rng.integers(0, 24, 5000))
df['hour_sin'], df['hour_cos'] = np.sin(2*np.pi*t/24), np.cos(2*np.pi*t/24)
x = pd.Series(rng.lognormal(size=5000))
df['x_clipped'] = x.clip(x.quantile(.01), x.quantile(.99))
